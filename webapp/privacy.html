Privacy

Chitti's whole design is built around keeping your data on the phone. Here's exactly what that means — what never leaves the device, the only three cases that touch the network, and precisely what the backend can see.

Stays on the phone

On-device intelligence

The language model, speech recognition, and notification processing all run on the phone.

Your documents

ID document scanning and parsing happen on-device; the vault is kept behind your fingerprint lock.

Touches the network — only these three cases

1. Sign-in

Handled via Firebase Authentication.

2. Optional encrypted backup

The phone derives a key from a password you choose (PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 → AES-256) and encrypts the backup before it leaves the device. The server stores ciphertext only, in MongoDB GridFS, plus the public KDF parameters (salt, iteration count) needed to re-derive the key. Each account keeps only its latest backup.

3. Safe Browsing link checks

Only the link itself is sent, to Google Safe Browsing.

What the backend can actually see

CategoryWhat is stored
Per accountFirebase uid, email address, whether it's verified, display name, sign-in provider, account creation time, and last login time.
Per backup (only if you make one)Its size, the SHA-256 of the ciphertext, upload time, KDF parameters (algorithm, iteration count, salt), and the device name you supplied.
Access boundaryEvery route acts only on the caller's own token's uid — one account can never reach another account's data.
Request logsOnly request id, method, URL, status, and timing — never headers or tokens, bodies, or backup contents.

If you forget your backup password, the backup can't be recovered.

That's the stated cost of end-to-end encryption — not even the server operator can decrypt it.

Your controls

Clear your data

Any kind of stored data, or all of it, can be cleared from Settings.

Fingerprint app lock

App lock can require your fingerprint.

Account deletion

Deleting your account removes the user record and the backup together.
